# Research Methodology

Research should answer a concrete planning question with decision-grade evidence.

1. Discover official and operational sources broadly enough to avoid a single-channel blind spot.
2. Capture a source observation: owner, exact URL, publication/effective/event dates, retrieval time, supported wording, and artifact reference.
3. Normalize only what the observation supports, without overwriting it.
4. Preserve publisher truth, observed onsite reality, and personal interpretation separately.
5. Express finite-event state explicitly: published, tentative, changed, canceled, contradicted, personally confirmed, observed onsite, or superseded.
6. Promote reviewed findings only through a controlled write with readback, then derive personal decisions and itinerary placement separately.

Personal observations and memories are private continuity, not published facts. Agent suggestions are proposals, not evidence. Stop when the planning decision is supported and remaining unknowns are named.

Prove one manual path end to end before automation: capture one official page, normalize a bounded claim, reconcile a change, and present it correctly. Official pages can conflict internally; provenance must preserve that rather than granting automatic consistency to the publisher.
